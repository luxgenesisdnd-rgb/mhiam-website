Classroom Management with Compassion: Tips from 20+ Years in Teaching

Friday, August 1, 2025

"Students are not a distraction from more important work; they are the most important work."– Dr. John B. King Jr.

As an educator with over 24 years of experience teaching in the Philippines, remote Alaska, and now in Texas I have learned that compassion is the most powerful classroom management tool we can use. It reaches beyond rules and routines and into the heart of what truly shapes behavior: connection, dignity, and belonging.

In this blog, I want to share real-life stories and research-based practices that have helped me manage behavior with empathy, especially in diverse and special education settings.

Know the Whole Child: Culture, Context, and Compassion

To manage a classroom effectively, you must know your students deeply—not just their behavior, but who they are.

"Before you can teach them, you have to reach them."

When I taught in a small Alaskan village, I learned to honor Indigenous values and family customs. In Texas, working with bilingual students, I’ve adapted strategies to support language differences and cultural transitions.

Tip: Create a “student snapshot” that includes their strengths, triggers, communication needs, and cultural background.

Be Proactive, Not Reactive

One of the best ways to prevent disruptions is to build a classroom structure that works for everyone. That includes visual supports, predictable routines, and clear expectations.

"An ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure."

Students with autism, ADHD, or emotional needs especially benefit from structure. When they feel safe and know what to expect, they are more likely to thrive.

Tip: Use visual schedules, behavior cue cards, and consistent routines. These support all learners, not just those with IEPs.

Discipline with Dignity

Discipline doesn't have to mean punishment. In fact, it shouldn't. It should be about teaching, healing, and growing.

"We must teach behavior the same way we teach math—step by step, with patience and feedback."

Instead of isolating or suspending students, I hold restorative conversations where students reflect on their actions and decide how to make things right. This has reduced repeat behaviors and built stronger relationships.

Tip: Create a "Reflection Corner" where students can cool down, reflect, and then rejoin the class with support not shame.

Teach Emotional Regulation Every Day

Students can’t learn if they’re dysregulated. Emotional intelligence is the foundation of classroom success.

"When a flower doesn’t bloom, you fix the environment in which it grows, not the flower."

That’s why I use calming corners, breathing exercises, and SEL check-ins throughout the day. Teaching students how to identify, express, and regulate emotions is just as important as teaching them to read.

Tip: Start the day with a “feelings check-in.” Teach calming strategies like deep breathing, stretching, or quiet drawing.

Focus on the Positive

Catch them being good. Celebrate effort. Acknowledge small wins.

"Children flourish in an atmosphere of encouragement." – Rudolf Dreikurs

Positive reinforcement is far more effective than consequences. In my classroom, we have a culture of encouragement—shout-outs, kindness cards, and team celebrations are part of our everyday routine.

Tip: Use a classroom recognition system that celebrates kindness, resilience, and teamwork—not just obedience.

Compassion is a Teaching Strength

In every classroom, from Manila to Alaska to Texas, one thing remains true: Compassion creates connection, and connection changes everything.

"Teach with empathy, lead with heart, and manage with vision. Compassion doesn’t make you soft—it makes you strong where it counts."

Compassionate classroom management:

  1. Builds trust and relationships
  2. Supports diverse and neurodiverse learners
  3. Reduces burnout and conflict
  4. Fosters a joyful, safe, and productive classroom
